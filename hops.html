What the system is for

HOPS turns fragmented operational information into a controlled decision workflow. It organizes readiness, blockers, sustainment, integration status, industrial constraints, external evidence, lessons learned, revalidation triggers, and fielding-path questions without allowing the software to make binding operational, legal, funding, acquisition, production, workforce, quality, configuration, or release decisions.

All portfolio examples are synthetic and unclassified. HOPS is an independent portfolio prototype and is not presented as a product, internal system, or endorsed capability of any employer or third party.

v2.6 EXTENSION · SYNTHETIC INDUSTRIAL READINESS

Distributed industrial-readiness planning

Configuration identity

Tracks synthetic platform/autonomy pairings so planning records preserve which configuration a capacity, workforce, spares, or acceptance signal applies to.

Capacity & workforce

Compares non-binding planning capacity with qualified-workforce coverage and unresolved constraints without turning the estimate into a production commitment.

Spares & suppliers

Surfaces critical-spares depth and synthetic supplier constraints as planning evidence rather than autonomous purchasing or sourcing authority.

Acceptance queues

Shows synthetic assets awaiting review or acceptance so queue pressure is visible while quality and acceptance authority remain human.

Reconstitution readiness

Packages recovery and reconstitution indicators for human comparison when the operating or industrial state changes.

Bounded demo state

The retained synthetic scenario exercises four industrial nodes, 36 units/month of non-binding aggregate planning capacity, three of four verified synthetic pairings, 15 synthetic queued assets, and four review findings. These figures are test inputs, not real production data.

Core capability lanes

Readiness & blockers

Combines readiness state, sustainment status, owners, dependencies, and unresolved blockers into a decision-oriented operating picture.

Decision Watch

Tracks assumptions and revalidation triggers so a decision can be reopened when the evidence, environment, or risk state changes.

Governed lessons

Captures operational lessons with provenance, review, promotion, recall, and lifecycle controls rather than treating every observation as trusted institutional knowledge.

Evidence normalization

Separates external or operational signals from conclusions, preserving provenance and uncertainty before they can influence a decision package.

Acquisition / fielding path

Screens candidate discussion paths and explicit barriers while keeping authority, competition, eligibility, funding, and award determinations with humans.

Security & audit

Uses input constraints, egress controls, authorization gates, audit-chain checks, hostile-input validation, and fail-visible review states.

Human authority is a design requirement

HOPS can aggregate, capture, screen, compare, explain, flag, forecast, and package information. It is deliberately prevented from approving configurations, releasing production, accepting assets, ordering spares, tasking a workforce, binding an organization, obligating funds, declaring legal eligibility, asserting competition is satisfied, or issuing mission tasking.

The acquisition/fielding and industrial-readiness lanes default unresolved questions to REVIEW REQUIRED and preserve explicit barriers when evidence or authority is incomplete.

Design rule: automation should reduce operator burden and improve decision quality without displacing accountable human judgment.
