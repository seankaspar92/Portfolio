The problem and my role

The problem

A recognized phrase is not an approved action. Operators need to inspect what was understood, withhold ambiguous requests, and distinguish dispatch from completion.

My role

I developed the prototype with AI assistance, defined operator authority boundaries, and organized the software and evaluation workflow around inspectable evidence.

Walk through one request

Illustrative reconstruction of the documented simulator flow
  1. Request

    An operator requests an assignment for Aircraft 3 and 5 in the synthetic fleet.

  2. Review

    The interface shows the interpreted aircraft and named route. The proposal waits for separate confirmation.

  3. Confirm

    The simulator rechecks proposal ownership, freshness, aircraft readiness, and the confirmation code.

  4. Observe

    Aircraft 3 can complete while Aircraft 5 rejects. Replay preserves both outcomes without executing them again.

What the consolidated prototype now includes

Target-bound platform gateway

Separate simulator and status targets preserve platform identity. Wrong-target, duplicate, expired and stale confirmations are withheld rather than silently routed elsewhere.

Confirmed local HOPS updates

Select a mission and platform scope, inspect blockers, propose an owner or status update, and confirm it separately. The SQLite workspace retains saved records and an audit trail.

Read-only adapter kit

Versioned manifests, field mapping, host-role checks and fixture conformance support status-source integration. External write adapters and real-source qualification are outside SDK v1.

Local launcher and review tools

Validated configuration, startup checks, stable data storage and bounded lifecycle logs sit alongside guided human trials, recording review and source citations.

The local HOPS workspace is separate from the hosted static HOPS demo. Recovery-source readers do not establish physical control or validated live telemetry.
